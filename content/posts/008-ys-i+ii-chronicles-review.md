**THIS REVIEW WILL CONTAIN SPOILERS FOR YS I+II CHRONICLES+**


*Quick review for those that want to stay spoiler free:*

*Even though the original Ys I+II released almost 40 years ago, these versions of the
games do not feel all that dated. Even though the two games have some 80s jank in
there they are still enjoyable today. The games combat and progression is simple yet
effective. If you are already a Ys fan or never played the series I think these are
worth picking up and playing. I really enjoyed these games. 8/10 for both.*

Falcom has become probably my favorite video game company. They really did hook me with
the Trails series and after catching up with Trails through Daybreak 2 (this was before
Horizon dropped in english) back in July I wanted to try out some of their other franchises.
So I decided to try out Ys VIII and I really enjoyed it. I put it on my backlog to try out some
of the other games in the franchise. I had it in my head that the next one I would play would be
X but I noticed that Ys I+II Chronicles and a few other of the older games were on sale for about $5
so I decided to pick a few up due to having a four day weekend off from work. Below is a screenshot of 
probably one of the most insane value Steam carts you'll ever see.

![The most awesome steam cart](/imgs/008/steamcart.png)

So with these games picked up, I played all of Ys I over my four day weekend and then played Ys II in short
burts throughout the week. The versions here that are on steam is the Chronicles+ version. Ys I+II has a lot of
different versions released on all sorts of systems but these seem to be the most modern ones. It is strange because
I assumed they'd be in a launcher with both games but nope it gives you both of the games seperately on Steam.

I played both of these games on nightmare and also got the platinum in both. This review will be divided into sections
unlike my last review where I just kind of yapped about what I liked and didn't.

## Story

The story of these games is pretty simple for the most part. You play as Adol and it follows his adventures through the island of
Esteria in I and then the floating island of Ys in II. His ship crashes and he washes up on the shore of Esteria which is currently
having some issues with demons.

In Ys I, you travel though the island looking for the ancient books of Ys which you later find out once all six are brought together 
they will have great power. The story of this game is really simple but its effective and gets the job done. In Ys II, a few minutes
after the end of I, Adol is sent up to the floating island of Ys. He finds out that people are being kidnapped and sacrificed. He
sets out to get to the bottom of this. You end up meeting some goddesses and other things go down. 

Ys I's story is really simple but Ys II's tells something more. Both complement each other really well. The structure reminds me of
Trails in the Sky FC and SC where concepts are explored and introduced in the first game then expanded upon in the second and Falcom
is really really good at writing stories like this.

I enjoyed the characters and plot a lot even if it was on the simpler side so that's really all I need to say about that.

## Gameplay

The combat in Ys I+II is what the game calls the bump system. Basically Adol automatically attacks enemies when he runs into this.
If you hit the enemy straight on you take damage. This combat is really simple but it is really statisfying once you get it down.
If you hit an enemy at a diagonal you basically can carry them across the screen and combo them till they die. You also can corner
enemies onto a wall for a death combo as well. The combat took a little bit of getting used to at first but once I had it down it
was really fun. Ys II introduces magic into the combat. There is one attacking spell that is a fireball. I found myself using the
fireball a lot more then the actual bump combat in Ys II so I will say that it was kind of unfortunate that it just kind of
overshadowed the core combat. In the earlier sections where you did not have much mana I was doing like run up to an enemy and combo
the fireball into bump hits but that became worse once I got the accessory that made fireballs track and got more mana.

The dungeon locations were pretty neat for the most part and I really enjoyed the progression system. It's very similar to the old
top down 2D Zelda games where you find a new item and it can unlock various new areas. For gear there's upgrades to your sword and
armor that basically just do more damage or have more defense. There's also accessories that give you various buffs. In the first
game they were simple like more damage, more defense, heal while standing still in dungeons, slower enemies, etc. Ys II's
accessories mainly revolved around the magic. The game also has a level system so as you level up you get more health, mana,
strength, and defense. I like the progression in this game because it always felt like you were improving something and every quest
item you picked up filled in a slot and seeing the inventory get more and more full as the game progressed was really satisfying.

The bosses in this game were honestly pretty challenging too. The ones in I were harder then II. There were a couple of bosses that
I really hated in Ys I but I will talk abou those in the section about things I did not like.

## Some Other Cool Things

I also just want to touch on some other cool things about the games that I enjoyed. For starters, I love the artstyle of the renders.
They have that late 2000s anime aesthetic and this is probably my favorite type of artstyle. I really wish we could go back to it. It
was cool because you could switch between two versions of renders one from the Complete versions of the game and ones from the
Chronicles. The complete renders were early 2000s so those also looked awesome but I played with the Chronicle ones. The amount of
settings in the game were cool.

Another thing I really found cool about these games is the history. Ys I+II have so many different versions because back in the day
for whatever reason games on different systems had weird differences. It was really cool to go back and see these older versions after
I would beat a boss or something and comparing the two versions. I really enjoy seeing the history of things like this so that was a
fun touch. If you decide to play these games I would recommend doing the same.

Final thing are some little details in the games. There are a couple of quests that have you escorting NPCs. A fun detail about these
are you can actually level up the NPCs if you force bump them into enemies. This was neat and had achievements tied to them. In Ys II,
there is a spell that turns you into a small demon. Enemies don't attack you while its active and you can actually talk to the demons.
It seems like every enemy spawn in the game had unique dialogue and each human NPC seemed to have unique dialogue if you talk to them
while in that form. The demon dialogue was fun to read at points and there was a common theme along all of them. They were either
scared of Adol or just really hungry... poor guys...

## Things I Didn't Enjoy

I really didn't have much that I didn't enjoy about these games only really two things I can think of off the top of my head.

The first is a small thing about the NPC following sections. THEY JUST MOVED TOO SLOW. [This video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chn0ggv9TdY) 
best described how I felt during these haha.

The really thing is there were two bosses in Ys I that were terrible. The first was this bat boss, Vagullion, who was the third boss. He would
split into a bunch of smaller bats and they would chase you around and then they would combine again and then you hit him. However,
the window where you hit him is really small so you need to do some crazy kiting to get good hits in. It was not an impossible boss
just too a really long time. If they made the hit window a bit longer it would probably be a less annoying fight. See the below
picture of my time attack times and you'll see why that really could be annoying.

![My time attack times](/imgs/008/times.jpg)

I also just want to complain about the final boss of Ys I. He was really damn hard. He floats around the screen really fast in the
DVD screensaver pattern and you need to hit him. He is constantly shooting fireballs at your location and when they miss you they
split into a ton of smaller fireballs that go everywhere. The smaller fireballs are really hard to dodge so basically you just
get hit by those. Dodging them would be possible if it wasn't for his other gimmick. Everytime you hit him it destroys the tile you
were standing on. So all of these combine just make for a really hard boss fight if you are standing on the tile that gets destroyed
you also instantly die so there were a lot of attempts where I got checkmated. This fight took me 6+ hours to beat but I will be
honest it never felt impossible. It was probably the difficulty scaling because on time attack its normal difficulty and I beat him
first try.

## My Final Thoughts

I really enjoyed Ys I+II. Even though the original versions of these games released 40 years ago and these are just more modern
versions of them you can tell they were really cooking with these games. I would gladly recommend these games to people who like
action RPGs. They also are not that long of games Ys I took me 18 hours and Ys II 13 hours but it could probably be completed a lot
faster if you weren't on nightmare or going for the platinum. As these two were the second and third Ys games I've played, I can see
why the series got popular and spawn many sequels. Thanks for reading my review and I will see you all in the next one!
