**THIS REVIEW WILL CONTAIN SPOILERS FOR LINK'S AWAKENING**

*They shouldn't be too major but if you care about that thing then here's the summary of my thoughts:*

*Link's Awakening (Switch) was a fun game that I happened to get for cheaper price at a second hand game store.
The dungeons were fun, the world was wacky and interesting, and overall I enjoyed the experience a lot. Traditional
Zelda games I enjoy way more then the newer open world styles of BOTW and TOTK. However, if you need to pay full
price for this game I would not recommend it.*

Link's Awakening has been on my radar for a long time. I enjoy Zelda games but I have not played a lot of them to completion.
I've only beaten Ocarina of Time, Link Between Worlds, Breath of the Wild, and Tears of the Kingdom. Getting to more Zelda games
has been on my list of things to play forever. I started playing Link's Awakening DX on the GameBoy Color on an emulator on my phone
during a car trip one time but I lost the save after that phone broke so I never finished it. Back before I bought my Switch 2, I
traded some games to a second hand game store and was able to pick up Link's Awakening, Xenoblade 1, and a Monkey Ball game for $50
which was such a steal. After beating one route of Fire Emblem Three Houses (I will be reviewing it when I complete the remaining 
two plus DLC) I decided to play this until Fortune's Weave drops because I did not want to burn out on Fire Emblem. 

This review is going to be pretty short because the game was not that long only took me about 10-12 hours to beat. 

## Gameplay

If you have never played a traditional Zelda game before heres the gist of it. You usually explore around an overworld usually in a linear
fashion going to different dungeons. Each dungeon has an item that it will grant you and that item is used in the dungeon to solve
puzzles. Sometimes dungeons will use other items as well to combine everything into satisfying puzzles. These items also unlock
different ways to explore around the overworld too so the progression becomes really satisfying.

Link's Awakening really shows how awesome the traditional style is. This was the fourth Zelda game that came out following up
Link to the Past. Also being the first Zelda game built for a handheld which was the Game Boy. The Game Boy is a pretty weak console
so its impressive they were able to fit a game of this scale onto that console.

There are eight main dungeons in the game with plenty of little areas that you can find in the overworld that have puzzles. One thing
that I liked about this game being a bit smaller scale is that it was easier to keep locations that I would need a later item to
complete the puzzle in my head. After getting a new item in a dungeon I was able to be like oh yeah there's this area in the map that
I can probably go explore now. So the progression was really satisfying. There is heart pieces and hidden seashells to find for
exploring. I got a lot of the heart pieces but did not find that many seashells. There also was some other things I missed like one
of the ocarina songs and also upgrades to the amount of bombs, arrows, and magic powder you could carry.

The dungeons in this game were really solid. There were a couple that involved multiple floors but for the most part there was only
one floor to a lot of the dungeons. My favorite was definitely the seventh dungeon Eagle Tower. This one involved a gimmick where you
had to being a giant metal ball around to knock the tower down to be able to progress. It was cool to plan out how to get the ball
around. A lot of the puzzles in the dungeon were really satisfying and I never really felt super stuck. There was a couple of times
I had to look up how to get to certain areas because the entrances were hidden under bushes I just didn't think to break but once I
caught onto that I didn't have to really look it up again. The bosses were fine. A lot of them felt really easy and did not last
very long. For example, I think I beat the first boss in like 10 seconds it was just too easy. The final boss was good though. 
The dungeon mini bosses were also really cool. There was one where you had to race the enemy to pick up a ball and picking that up
and throwing it at the enemy was the only way to kill it. That one was fun because you could combo it and it was really satisfying to
do that. 

The game has all the classic Zelda items being the pegasus boots, a bow, the hookshot, a boomerang, bombs, and a power bracelet were
here and were fun to use. An item I liked that isn't in the other traditional Zelda games I've played being the Roc's Feather which
allows you to jump was there. This was just fun to use and jump around with I had it equipped most of the game. There also is an extra
dungeon that was added in the Game Boy Color release called the Color Dungeon that's back in this game. Beating this dungeon let's
you get an upgrade to Link's tunic. You can either pick red or blue. The red tunic gives you double damage and the blue tunic which
cuts damage in half. I picked the red one because I didn't die up to that point and ended up not dying the entire game. I think these
two upgrade completely break the game. There are pick ups that you find throughout the game that give you those perks for like 30
seconds and they are good but having one of them permanently just made it too easy. I remember Link Between Worlds having sword and
tunic upgrades for damage and damage taken but the enemies would scale with that so you would end up find.

Finally, I want to talk a bit about the Switch improvements over the Game Boy versions. Obviously there's big improvements to the
controls because there is more then two face buttons this time around. It was so annoying playing the originals and your sword and
shield had to be swapped around so often you had to spend a lot of time so sword, shield, and pegasus boots having dedicated button
is so nice. There also is a new NPC, Dampe the grave keeper, who has a new collectable being chamber stones. These chamber stones let
you build your own dungeons. I did not interact with this system much because you could not download or play others dungeons.
Apparently there's some premade ones but I did not check those out maybe I will go back and do it someday.

There also is two animated cutscenes that have a really nice anime art style for the intro and
ending. I wish that they would make a Zelda game in that style I'm sure it would look so nice.

## Story, Characters, and Koholint Island

Before I end this review I quickly want to talk about the story, characters, and the location of the game. The story is really simple,
Link is just trying to get off the island and its all a dream so once he wakes up everything will be gone. The characters on the
island were interesting. Marin is a good character even though she only gets a few scenes. The ones she gets are good though. Ballad
of the Wind Fish is also such a catchy tune too and really sticks with you.

I really like how wacky Koholint Island is too. Because its a dream there is just lots of weird stuff here. Singing frogs, an animal
village, random Mario enemies, and even Kirby as an enemy in one dungeon. This just really made the location memorable.

## Conclusion

Overall, I really enjoyed Link's Awakening. It was a really fun game with some great puzzles and a nice location. I would give it an 8 out of 10. However, I do not think this game is worth it if you need to buy it at full price. I would say it's probably worth like 
$30-$40 so if you can get it for that then definitely play it. Unforunately that's just the Nintendo tax for you. Anyway, thanks for
reading my review and I will see you all in the next one.
